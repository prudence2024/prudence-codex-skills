01.  What workflow tools are out there? 8 min
Section 1: What Workflow Tools Are Out There?
Building an app isn’t just about what happens inside the app but it’s also about connecting it to the 
outside world. Workflow automation tools let you link your app with other services and tools, so things 
can happen automatically in the background. For example, when someone signs up on your site, a 
workflow could send them an email or text message with a special offer availble for a short term to 
get the user to convert to a customer very quickly.
Think of these workflows as a series of connected steps: something happens (like a form is 
submitted), and then one or more actions take place across the apps and platforms you use. These 
automations help keep your data organized, save you time, and open up new possibilities for how 
your app works with the rest of your tech stack.
We’ll go into specific examples and the power of these automations in the next section. But first, let’s 
get familiar with the main tools that make these workflows possible.
At the time of this writing, AI agents are starting to do some of this work themselves but they're still a 
bit more complex.  We discuss them in a later module you can go read.
The Main Workflow Automation Tools
There are a handful of workflow automation platforms that have become industry standards. Each 
one has its own strengths, style, and sweet spot. Here are the three most popular options you’ll see 
non-coders and developers alike using:
The Big Three Workflow Automation Platforms: Deeper Dive
1. Zapier
Overview:
Zapier is the most recognized name in workflow automation. It’s built for simplicity and works with 
thousands of apps right out of the box.
Strengths:
• Extremely easy to use: Great for beginners, with a clean and friendly interface.
• Huge integration library: Over 5,000 apps supported, including almost all major SaaS products.
• Reliability: Known for uptime and consistent execution.
• Lots of templates: Quickly get started with pre-made “Zaps” for common use cases.
• Great documentation and help articles.
Weaknesses:

• Limited customization: Some advanced logic (like complex branching or looping) is only 
available on higher plans, or not possible at all.
• Pricing: Can get expensive as you scale up your automations and require more “tasks” per 
month.
• Data handling: Not as robust for complex data transformations or multi-step workflows.
• No on-premise/self-hosting option.
Pricing:
• Free tier: Up to 100 tasks/month, single-step Zaps only.
• Starter: ~$20/month, up to 750 tasks/month, multi-step Zaps.
• Professional: ~$50/month, up to 2,000 tasks/month, more features like paths (conditional logic).
• Team & Company: Higher tiers for teams, with advanced permissions and higher usage limits.
• Zapier Pricing
Support:
• Email support, fast responses on paid plans.
• Extensive help center, user community, and how-to guides.
Review Scores (2025):
• G2: 4.5/5 (based on thousands of reviews)
• Capterra: 4.7/5
2. Make
Overview:
Make offers more control and flexibility than Zapier, with a beautiful visual editor and powerful data 
handling tools. It’s a favorite for users who want to go beyond the basics.
Strengths:
• Visual workflow builder: Drag-and-drop interface for mapping out complex processes.
• Advanced logic: Filters, routers (branching), iterators, and more for handling real-world 
workflows.
• Great value: More features at a lower price point than many competitors.
• Detailed data manipulation: Map, filter, and transform data as it moves between apps.
Weaknesses:
• Learning curve: Can feel overwhelming for total beginners.

integrations take longer to appear.
• Support can be slow: Especially on lower-priced or free plans.
• No self-hosting option.
Pricing (2025):
• Free tier: Up to 1,000 operations/month, limited features.
• Core: ~$10/month, up to 10,000 operations/month.
• Pro: ~$16/month, up to 10,000 operations/month, priority support.
• Teams & Enterprise: Higher limits and features for larger orgs.

• Capterra: 4.7/5
3. N8N
Overview:
N8N is an open-source workflow automation tool that gives you the option to host it yourself for 
maximum control, privacy, and customizability. It’s ideal for technical users and teams who want 
flexibility.
Strengths:
• Open source: No vendor lock-in, modify the code if you want.
• Self-hosting: Run on your own servers or cloud (great for privacy, compliance, or custom setups).
• Advanced workflows: Branching, loops, custom code nodes, and error handling.
• Extensible: Build your own connectors or use community-created nodes.
• Free to use if self-hosted.
Weaknesses:
• Requires setup: Self-hosting means you (or your tech team) manage installation and updates.
• User interface: Has improved a lot, but can still feel less polished than Zapier or Make.

• Support: Limited on the free, self-hosted plan; paid cloud plans get more support.
Pricing (2025):
• Self-hosted: Free (open source).
• Cloud: Starts at ~$20/month (includes hosting, updates, and basic support).
• Enterprise: Custom plans for large teams.

02.  The power of what you can do with workflows. 10 min
Section 2: The Power of Automation Workflows
Automating workflows is about making your app and your tools work together, seamlessly. When you 
connect services with automation, you stop spending time on repetitive tasks and let technology 
handle routine operations for you. Not only does this save you time, but it also helps you keep your 
data consistent, respond faster to your users, and build smarter systems that scale easily.
Let’s go deeper on how this works in practice, using three detailed, real-world examples withone 
each for Zapier, Make, and N8N.
Example 1: Automatically Create a Support Ticket from App User Requests
(Using Zapier)
Scenario:
Imagine a user submits a support or bug report from your app (maybe through a contact form or 
feedback button). Y ou want every submission to instantly create a ticket in your helpdesk tool (like 
Zendesk, Freshdesk, or Jira), so nothing ever gets lost in your inbox.
How to Build It in Zapier:
Step 1: Set Up the Trigger
• In Zapier, create a new Zap.
• Choose “Webhooks by Zapier” as your trigger app.
• Select the “Catch Hook” event. Zapier will generate a unique webhook URL.
• In your app, configure the support/feedback form to send submissions (via POST) to this Zapier 
webhook URL.
(So with AI, prompt:“Generate a feedback form that POSTs to this webhook URL: [paste URL 
here]. Include fields for user email, subject, and message.”)
Step 2: Connect to Y our Helpdesk Tool
• Add an Action step to your Zap and choose your support platform (Zendesk, Freshdesk, Jira, 
etc.).
• Select the “Create Ticket” or equivalent action.
Step 3: Map the Form Data
• Map the fields from your webhook (user email, subject, message) to the correct fields in your 
support tool.
Step 4: Test Y our Zap

--- PDF PAGE 7 ---
• Use a test submission from your app to make sure the data flows through and a support ticket 
appears in your system.
Step 5: Turn On Y our Zap
• Once everything works, turn the Zap on.
• Now, every user issue is automatically tracked and assigned so no more digging through emails!
Extra Tips:
• Add a Slack notification or email to your team as a second Action step if you want real-time 
alerts.
Example 2: Get Notifications in Slack When a Payment is Received
(Using Make.com)
Scenario:
Y ou want your team to get a Slack message every time a user pays through Stripe. That way, you 
never miss a sale, and your customer service can react right away.
How to Build It in Make:
Step 1: Start a New Scenario
• In Make, click “Create a new scenario.”
• Add a Stripe module as the first step, using the “Watch Events” or “Watch Payments” trigger.
• Connect your Stripe account.
Step 2: Add a Slack Module
• After the payment trigger, add a Slack “Send a Message” module.
• Connect your Slack account and select the desired channel.
Step 3: Map the Data
• Drag and drop fields from Stripe (payer’s name, amount, time, etc.) into your Slack message.
Step 4: Test the Scenario
• Trigger a test payment to ensure the Slack message appears as expected.
Step 5: Schedule and Activate

--- PDF PAGE 8 ---
• Set the scenario to run automatically.
• Now, you get instant Slack updates for every successful payment.
Extra Tips:
• Use filters to only notify for payments over a certain amount, or add more steps to update a 
database or send a customer email.
Example 3: Sync Data Between Your App and a Google Sheet for Reporting
(Using N8N)
Scenario:
Y ou want your app’s data (like new user signups, bug reports, or feature requests) to be synced to a 
Google Sheet, so it’s easy to analyze or share with team members.
How to Build It in N8N:
Step 1: Create the Trigger
• Start a new workflow in N8N.
• Add a webhook node or another trigger node (like a database poll).
Step 2: Add Google Sheets Node
• Add a Google Sheets “Add Row” node.
• Authenticate your Google account.
Step 3: Map Data
• Map fields from your trigger (user data, timestamp, etc.) to the columns in your Google Sheet.
Step 4: Test and Activate
• Run a test to ensure data lands in your Sheet as expected.
• Activate the workflow.
Extra Tips:
• Add additional logic—like data validation or branching—before sending to Google Sheets.
• N8N’s visual editor lets you see the entire workflow at a glance.

03.  Simple workflow example for beta testing 10 min
Section 3: Simple Workflow Example – Beta Signup Flow
Automating your beta signup process is one of the most powerful and practical ways to see workflow 
automation in action. This example not only reduces your manual workload, but also keeps your early-
access users organized and makes it easy to move them through your approval process. Let’s walk 
through how you can set this up using Make.com with just a few building blocks.
Overview: How the Beta Signup Workflow Works
Here’s what you’ll accomplish:
1. A user signs up for beta access on your site.
Their info (like email and name) gets sent to a webhook endpoint.
2. That info is saved as a new row in a Google Sheet.
This sheet becomes your master list for everyone interested in the beta.
3. When you’re ready, you add users to your Supabase whitelist.
This lets you invite users manually and control who gets access.
This workflow keeps everything organized, avoids unnecessary noise for users you’re not ready to 
onboard, and makes it easy to audit and communicate with your beta testers.
Preparation: What You Need to Set Up First
Before you build the automation in Make.com (or Zapier/N8N), it helps to have your tools and 
accounts ready:
1. Create Your Google Sheet
• Make a new Google Sheet, such as “Beta Signups.”
• Add columns you’ll want to track—at minimum, something like:
Email, Name, Signup Date, Status.
• Leave the sheet empty; the workflow will fill it in as users sign up.
2. Create Your Supabase Whitelist Table
• In your Supabase dashboard, set up a table for beta users so name it something like 
beta_whitelist.  (Hint! Ask AI to give you the SQL code to create this)
• Add columns matching your Google Sheet (for easy mapping), such as:
email, name, invited_at, status.
• This table controls which users are allowed to access your app in beta.
3. Authenticate Your Accounts in Make.com

--- PDF PAGE 11 ---
• In Make.com, connect your Google account (so you can read/write to Sheets).
• Connect your Supabase project by creating a Supabase connection where this will typically ask 
for your project URL and API key.
• These connections let Make.com talk to your spreadsheet and your database.
4. (Optional but Helpful) Prepare Your Beta Signup Form
• Use Cursor to quickly generate a signup form that will POST data to a webhook URL (you’ll get 
this from Make.com in the next step).
Building the Workflow: Step by Step in Make.com
Step 1: Create a Webhook in Make.com
• In Make.com, start a new Scenario and add the “Webhooks” module.
• Choose “Custom Webhook” and create a new webhook and Make.com will give you a unique 
URL.
• This URL is where your beta signup form should POST the user’s data.
• With AI:
Use a prompt like:“Generate a signup form that sends email and name to this webhook URL: 
[paste your Make.com webhook URL here].”
Step 2: Add Google Sheets to Capture Signups
• After the webhook module, add the “Google Sheets > Add a Row” module.
• Select the Google Sheet you prepared earlier (“Beta Signups”).
• Map each form field (email, name, signup date) from the webhook to the correct columns in your 
spreadsheet.
• This way, every signup submission instantly becomes a new row in your sheet.
Step 3: Add Supabase to Handle Your Whitelist
• Add the “Supabase > Create a Row” module to your Scenario.
• Select your beta_whitelist table.
• Map the relevant fields (email, name, etc.) from the webhook data or Google Sheet row.
• Y ou can choose to automate this for every signup, or set a filter so only approved users get 
whitelisted.

Why This Approach Works
• Control: Y ou can keep public signups closed and only onboard users you select from your list.
• Organization: All your signups are tracked in one place, making it easy to review, sort, or export 
as needed.
• Scalability: Y ou can trigger email notifications, invitations, or even user creation in Supabase all 
from your beta list, and all without manual entry.
• Auditability: Y ou always know who signed up, who was approved, and when.
