Budgeting & Cost Management
Before we start crunching numbers, it helps to get a bird’s-eye view of where money actually 
flows when you build and run a web app. Once you’ve got that, you can run “what-if” 
scenarios, put guardrails around runaway costs, and even optimise your architecture so you 
get more bang for every pound or dollar you spend.

01.  Where will you spend money? 8 min
Every successful project begins with a clear ledger. Before you spin up servers or sign up for a fancy 
design tool, you need to know every place you might spend money. Sketching this cost map up front 
gives you the context to trim or defer expenses later and avoids those heart-stopping surprises when 
your first cloud bill arrives.
Domains
Y our domain is where people find you and what users type into their browsers. A memorable, 
branded domain (for example, myapp.com) instantly looks more professional than a long subdomain. 
That said, during early development you can often get away with a free subdomain from Vercel 
(myapp.vercel.app) or Netlify (myapp.netlify.app).
Cloudflare is your friend!  Easy to use, generous free tier, cheap pricing, and added options like 
DDoS protection, DNSSec, and so much more.  It's my go to for managing all my domains.
SSL certificates, the little padlock in the address bar, are non-negotiable. Without HTTPS your site 
will be flagged as insecure in modern browsers and your users won’t trust you with their data. Luckily 
Let’s Encrypt provides certificates at no cost as do platforms like Vercel manage renewal 
automatically.  
Servers
Y our front-end assets (HTML, CSS, JavaScript) and back-end logic (APIs, scheduled jobs, 
databases) each need a home. Picking the right hosting platform affects performance, scale and your 
wallet.

A CDN (content delivery network) like CloudFront or Vercel’s edge network brings your assets closer 
to users around the globe. Caching static files aggressively can slash bandwidth costs and speed up 
your site. Early on you might start on the free tier of Vercel or Netlify, then later switch to a paid CDN 
plan once committed-use discounts make per-GB fees cheaper.  Many websites or apps may not 
need a CDN.  It just depends on if people are going to be pulling content from you or not and you 
need a reliable place to store all these assets.

Serverless functions (Lambda) let you pay only for execution time, but they can introduce latency 
when they “cold start.” Fixed-size droplets or dynos give you a predictable monthly bill but cost you 
even when traffic is low. For an MVP , a small Railway or Heroku dyno is perfect. As you grow, 
consider reserved instances or container services with savings plans to lower unit costs.
Prompt to try in AI:
“Generate a markdown file of front-end and back-end providers with their free-tier limits, startup 
costs and bandwidth allowances so we can align what might work best with our requirements 
defined in our PRD. Go do some web searching to get the latest pricing.”
Database & Auth
Y our users’ data like profiles, preferences, content must live somewhere. Managed solutions like 
Supabase, Firebase or MongoDB Atlas bundle storage, real-time sync and authentication into one 
package. They often include a generous free tier (for example, 500 MB storage and 10 000 monthly 
active users) but then charge per additional GB stored or per 10 000 reads and writes.
Two cost factors to watch: the amount of data you store and the number of operations you perform. 
Egress fees for pulling data out of your database or storage can also sneak up on you. For tiny MVPs 
you might even get away with SQLite behind a serverless function or static JSON files if you don’t 
need real-time updates.  At a minimum, Supabase will cost $10 per instance, and you'll likely need a 
dev and prod instance post MVP stage. 
Marketing & Email
Getting and keeping users engaged means sending newsletters, drip campaigns and transactional 
emails (password resets, receipts). Platforms like Brevo and Mailchimp charge by the number of 
contacts or emails sent are often free up to 1 000 contacts, then £0.001–£0.003 per email beyond.  
Brevo does have a good free tier though which includes 300 emails a day.  In addition there are some 
good CRM features and it's easy to integrate into your code with the use of a single API key put into 
your environment variables.
If you only send a handful of transactional emails, Brevo works great for this as they will provide you 
with an SMTP server in that free tier too!

Design Tools
High-fidelity mockups in Figma or Framer speed development. But each seat can cost £12–£20 per 
month once you add collaboration features. Early on you can sketch in free tools like Penpot or even 
Google Slides. As your UI stabilises, you might cancel extra seats or switch to a cheaper plan.  Canva 
even has a free plan that will help you design some basic graphics.  The point is options are out ther 
to do this on a budget!  Then you have free icon libraries like Shadcn that can be used in your code 
which Cursor will know about.  
AI & LLM Tokens
If your app invokes large language models the token tally translates directly into cash. Base models 
can run at around £0.002 per 1 000 tokens while advanced variants cost £0.02 or more for the same 
volume. Implement prompt caching to avoid duplicate calls and batch related requests whenever 
possible. During development you can lean on smaller, local open-source LLMs to validate prompts 
before switching to a paid API.  Deepseek is incredibly good value for what you get.  If you are really 
savvy you can run the model yourself if you have the right hardware!
Third-Party APIs and Automation Tools
Anything you don’t build yourself like payment gateways (Stripe), geocoding (Google Maps), SMS 
(Twilio), data scraping (Apify) usually operates on pay-as-you-go pricing. A typical SMS API might 
charge £0.05 per message while a scraping run on Apify could be £0.01. Always develop on sandbox 
or free tiers, then swap to production keys at launch. If volume surges, look for volume-discount 
thresholds or open-source self-hosted replacements.
The same goes for Automation tools like make.com or n8n.org. They are nice free tiers which may be 
enough for what you need or a great for the MVP .  Beyond that though you will need to study their 
pricing structure.  N8N is unique in that you can host it yourself on your own server and run your own 
automations that way!  If you can pay for the hardware you don't have to pay per transaction! 
Storage
Object storage platforms (AWS S3, Google Cloud Storage, Supabase Storage) charge per GB stored 
plus fees for data retrieval and egress. Cold storage (archival) is cheaper per GB but slower and 
incurs retrieval charges. Hot storage keeps frequently accessed files ready but costs more. Clean up 
unused assets regularly, compress images and enable caching headers to keep your storage bill 
under control.

AI to help you figure this out, you will be well on your way to finding the balanced approach.  
Remember, it's not a bad thing to have usage costs if that means you've got lots of paying customers!

03.  Spotting and Taming Runaway Costs
3. Spotting and Taming Runaway Costs
Traffic spikes are thrilling until your cloud bill arrives. An unguarded API loop or a sudden surge in 
requests can turn growth into a budget disaster. In this section we’ll look at the common “cost 
exploits,” then build in guardrails so that every spike stays within predictable limits.  There are 
protections on both sides, in your app and also in the cloud platforms you leverage.  While we focus 
on your app in this section, be sure to take the time to study what alerts and monitoring tools are 
available to you on the cloud side as well.  Just ask the AI for help!
3.1 Identify “Cost Exploits”
First, audit your code and workflows for places where usage can balloon unexpectedly. Common 
culprits include:
• Infinite loops or recursive calls. A bug that retries a failed LLM call forever can rack up 
thousands of token requests in seconds.
• Large file uploads or downloads. Letting users upload multi-hundred-megabyte documents or 
images can blow past your storage and egress allowances.
• Batch operations without safeguards. A “bulk rewrite” feature that processes dozens of CVs in 
one go can be handy but also dangerously costly if misused.
Action: Review your most expensive endpoints (LLM calls, database writes, file transfers) and add 
simple counters or log-level monitoring so you can see when one user or process is responsible for 
90% of the usage.  Of course if you don't know what those are do an analysis with AI!  Have a 
discussion going through your documentation, the flow of key files, and see wher things can go 
haywire.
3.2 Rate-Limiting & Throttling at the Edge
Once you know where the danger zones are, you can throttle traffic before it even hits your core 
systems. We covered rate-limiting in an earlier module so it's good to recap some strategiees here.

Real World Example
In one of my apps, I built a queue system that allowed me to see what jobs were going into the queue 
and more importantly not overlaod my APIs as some of them were bound by how much RAM I was 
getting from the API provider.  Each job took up 4GB of RAM and I was given 8GB so I could only run 
2 jobs to that API at a time.  The global queue system allowed me to simply queue up jobs for this 
API provider so only 2 run at any given time preventing using more RAM and incurring more costs.
3.3 Real-Time Cost Alerts
Rate-limits catch over-use at the edge, but you’ll also want a watchtower on your overall spend. Set 
up alerts so you never get surprised by a 10× daily surge:
1. Cost API polling. Many platforms (Vercel, Railway, AWS Cost Explorer) offer billing or usage 
APIs.  Ask the AI to plug into them and give you a dashboard with an overview.
2. Threshold rules. Choose sensible trigger points e.g. when your month-to-date spend hits 50%, 
80% and 95% of budget.
3. Notification channels. Push alerts to Slack, email, or SMS so you or your team can jump in 
immediately.  Bonus:  Program in some automatic changes to stop the bleeding.

04.  Post-Launch Cost Audit: Cutting the Fat
4. Post-Launch Cost Audit: Cutting the Fat
Once CVPolish is live and real users are polishing their resumes, you’ll have concrete data on which 
services are earning their keep and which you can slim down or retire altogether. A regular cost audit 
lets you reclaim budget from under-used add-ons and allocate it where it really drives value.  This 
section is going to seem like the most basic but it's really all common sense and a fresh reminder to 
check your subscriptions!  Are you paying for things you simply don't need anymore or as much of?
4.1 Review Usage Dashboards for Under-Utilized Services
Head into each platform’s dashboard like Railway, Supabase, Vercel, and all those design-tool admin 
panels.  Look for services you’re paying for but not fully using.
• Database storage vs. reads/writes. Are you paying for a larger storage tier?
• Authentication seats. If you bought higher grade Supabase hardware tier, can you lower it 
safely?
• Design-tool licences. Still using that Canva subscription or can you go down to the free tier now?
• Add-ons and plugins. CVPolish might have enabled error-tracking or performance monitoring 
add-ons on every environment. Do you need them on staging or just production?

4.3 Migrate to Self-Hosted or Open-Source Where It Makes Sense
Some paid services are invaluable during early development, but once you’ve ironed out your 
workflows, open-source or self-hosted alternatives can save money:
• Example: Error Tracking. Sentry’s paid plan may cost $29/month; migrating to an open-source 
tool like Exceptionless on a small DigitalOcean droplet could run under $5/month.
• Example: Email Relay. If you’re only sending a couple of hundred transactional emails daily, 
Brevo let's you send 300 free emails a day. 
• Example: Analytics. Swap out a paid SaaS analytics tool for a self-hosted Matomo or Plausible 
instance once your tracking requirements stabilize.  Just run it in Railway next to your other 
services?

4.4 Re-Evaluate Your LLM Tier other other coding app tiers
Early on, CVPolish leaned heavily on AI assistance so you chose a higher tier for your model of 
choice (eg. Claude Code Max). Now that the core features are in place and you’re comfortable with 
the platform:
• Check your AI-assist usage. Look at how many hours or credits you actually consumed last 
month.
• Consider a smaller plan + top-up credits. Maybe you can downgrade now to the basic Pro plan.
• Reassess regularly. As your team grows or development slows, dial your plan up or down to 
match real needs.
It's all common sense remember!  Over the course of a year, a few tweaks and cuts here and there 
could pay for a nice Christmas party at the end of the year!
