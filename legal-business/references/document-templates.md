# Legal and business starting templates

> These are starting templates, not a substitute for a lawyer reviewing the final documents before they go live or are signed. Obtain Nigeria-qualified and, where applicable, cross-border counsel for Nigeria Data Protection Act/NDPR, GDPR/UK GDPR, consumer, sector-specific, tax, and contract issues.

Replace every bracketed field. Delete inapplicable options rather than leaving contradictory clauses.

## 1. Terms of Service

**Parties and acceptance.** These Terms govern access to `[Product]`, provided by `[Legal Entity, registration details, address]`. State when acceptance occurs and the effective/version date.

**Eligibility and accounts.** Define age/capacity requirements, account accuracy, credential security, and responsibility for authorized users.

**Service and licence.** Describe the service accurately, the limited right to use it, ownership of the platform, and treatment of customer content and feedback.

**Acceptable use.** Prohibit unlawful use, abuse, unauthorized access, malware, scraping beyond permitted limits, rights infringement, interference, and attempts to bypass security or usage controls.

**Fees and changes.** State billing cycle, taxes, renewal, price-change notice, suspension, and cancellation rules. Align with the Refund Policy.

**Availability and changes.** Explain maintenance, feature evolution, dependencies, and any applicable service-level commitment without making unsupported guarantees.

**Liability and warranties.** Add jurisdiction-appropriate disclaimers, exclusions, and liability caps. Mark consumer-law carve-outs and all exclusions/caps `[LAWYER REVIEW REQUIRED]`.

**Termination.** Cover suspension/termination grounds, notice, data export/deletion, accrued payment duties, and surviving clauses.

**Disputes and governing law.** Insert negotiation/escalation, courts or arbitration, venue, governing law, and mandatory consumer rights. Mark `[LAWYER REVIEW REQUIRED]`.

**Contact and notices.** Provide legal notice channels and rules for electronic notices.

## 2. Privacy Policy

**Controller identity and contacts.** Identify the responsible entity, privacy contact, and representative/DPO if legally required.

**Data inventory.** List only data actually collected: account identifiers, contact and delivery details, transaction metadata, user content, support messages, device/log data, analytics, cookies, and marketing preferences. State that payment card data is handled directly by the payment provider if that is true.

**Purpose and legal basis.** Map each data category to a specific purpose and lawful basis where applicable: contract, consent, legal obligation, legitimate interests, or another valid basis.

**Sources and sharing.** Name categories of subprocessors/recipients and link a maintained subprocessor list. Explain government/legal disclosures and business transfers.

**International transfers.** Identify hosting/processing regions and the transfer safeguard actually used. Mark cross-border mechanism `[LAWYER REVIEW REQUIRED]`.

**Retention.** Give defensible periods or decision criteria for accounts, orders, support, logs, backups, and legal records.

**Rights and deletion.** Explain access, correction, deletion, objection/restriction, portability, consent withdrawal, complaint channels, identity verification, response process, and legal exceptions.

**Security, children, cookies, updates.** Describe measures at an appropriate level without guaranteeing absolute security. Include children and cookie/analytics sections when relevant, plus version/change notice.

## 3. Data Processing Agreement (B2B)

**Roles and scope.** Identify controller/customer and processor/provider, subject matter, duration, purpose, data subjects, data types, and processing operations in an annex.

**Instructions and confidentiality.** Process personal data only on documented lawful instructions and bind authorized personnel to confidentiality.

**Security.** Attach proportionate technical and organizational measures covering access control, encryption, secure development, logging, vulnerability management, backups, recovery, and incident response.

**Subprocessors.** Provide authorization model, notice period for changes, objection process, and flow-down obligations.

**Assistance.** Cover data-subject requests, assessments/consultations, audits, regulator inquiries, and return/deletion at termination.

**Incidents.** Define notification without undue delay, required details, investigation cooperation, and evidence preservation. Do not promise a deadline the response process cannot meet.

**Transfers and liability.** State valid transfer mechanisms and how DPA liability interacts with the MSA. Mark jurisdictional language `[LAWYER REVIEW REQUIRED]`.

## 5. Master Service Agreement (MSA)

Include parties, order-of-precedence, statements of work/order forms, fees/taxes, invoicing and disputes, customer dependencies, change control, intellectual property, customer data, confidentiality, security, DPA incorporation, subcontractors, warranties, indemnities, liability caps/exclusions, insurance, term/termination, exit assistance/data return, publicity, assignment, force majeure, notices, governing law, and dispute resolution.

Attach a service-level schedule:

| Item | Commitment | Measurement/exclusion | Remedy/escalation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Monthly uptime | `[realistic %]` | Define monitoring, maintenance, customer/vendor exclusions | `[credit/remedy]` |
| Critical incident response | `[time]` | Define severity and business hours/24x7 | `[escalation]` |
| High-priority response | `[time]` | Define severity | `[escalation]` |
| Support hours | `[timezone/hours]` | Holidays and channels | `[after-hours rule]` |
| Recovery targets | `RTO [x] / RPO [y]` | Per covered service | `[continuity process]` |

All indemnities, liability provisions, governing law, dispute mechanisms, and SLA remedies require legal and operational approval.

## 6. Cyber-liability insurance readiness checklist

Prepare evidence, not just yes/no answers:

- asset inventory, data classification, and named security ownership;
- MFA for privileged, remote, cloud, email, and production access;
- least privilege, joiner/mover/leaver process, and periodic access reviews;
- supported systems, patch/vulnerability SLAs, scanning, and penetration testing;
- endpoint protection/EDR, secure configuration, and device management;
- encryption in transit/at rest and managed key/secret rotation;
- protected, tested, offline/immutable backups and documented RTO/RPO;
- centralized logging, alerting, monitoring coverage, and retention;
- written incident-response plan, contacts, tabletop exercises, and 48-hour post-mortems;
- vendor due diligence, contracts/DPAs, subprocessor inventory, and concentration risk;
- secure SDLC, reviews, dependency scanning, CI/CD controls, and environment separation;
- email security, phishing training, payment-change verification, and fraud controls;
- privacy inventory, retention/deletion process, breach assessment, and regulatory contacts;
- business continuity/disaster recovery exercises;
- prior incidents/claims and remediation evidence;
- requested limits, retention/deductible, territories, retroactive date, exclusions, social-engineering/ransomware/privacy coverage, business interruption, vendor outage, legal response, forensics, notification, and regulatory defense.

Have the broker and qualified counsel review proposal answers. Inaccurate answers can affect coverage or claims.
